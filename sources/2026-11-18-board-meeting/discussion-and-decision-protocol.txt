90-Minute Public Discussion
Discussion Guidelines

With 14 board members, board leadership will use a structured “round robin” format to ensure
everyone has an opportunity to participate.

   ●​ Ashley calls on each member individually, with no interruptions or responses until
      everyone has spoken.
   ●​ Rotate the starting member for each round.
   ●​ Allow approximately 2 minutes per member during each round.
   ●​ Encourage members to build on previous comments rather than repeat points already
      made.
   ●​ Factual questions can be directed to the administration for clarification.

The discussion is intended to help members understand the evidence, identify differences in
judgment, and explain their reasoning. Formal motions will be considered later under Action
Items.

1. Frame the Discussion (0–5 minutes)

Ashley’s Opening Remarks:

"Tonight we will be discussing whether the board should formally consider the closure of one or
more schools through the public hearing process outlined in our Articles of Agreement.
Our discussions to date have focused primarily on configurations in which Fayston and either
Moretown or Waitsfield would be considered for closure, with the remaining Valley schools and
Brookside serving PreK–4 students and grades 5 and 6 moving to Crossett Brook.

I want to emphasize that we are not voting tonight to close any schools. A final closure
decision, if it comes before the board, would be tentatively scheduled for January 6, following
the required public hearings.

The ratings and walkthroughs Lisa developed were intended to provide information to
guide our judgment, not produce a mathematical answer. Ultimately, it is the
responsibility of this board to consider what is best for HUUSD as a whole.

I recognize that members may reasonably reach different conclusions from the same
information. Our purpose tonight is to understand where the evidence points, where members
see different tradeoffs, and why they reach their conclusions.

To ensure everyone has an opportunity to participate, Cindy and I will guide the discussion
through three rounds of questions. We will ask everyone to speak once before anyone speaks a
second time.

While much of our discussion has centered on the possibility of closing two schools,
that is not a predetermined outcome. Our responsibility tonight is to consider the information
and determine what, if anything, should move forward for further consideration.

We will not be taking motions or votes during this discussion. Those opportunities will come later
in the meeting under Action Items."

2. Review the Evidence (5–20 minutes)

Ashley and Cindy will briefly summarize the ratings of the possible two-school configurations
and the Moretown and Waitsfield walkthrough findings.

The focus should be on:

   ●​   Significant differences among the configurations.
   ●​   Verified information about enrollment, capacity, facilities, and operations.
   ●​   Areas where estimates or assumptions remain.
   ●​   Considerations that may not be fully reflected in the ratings.

The objective is not to revisit every individual score but to establish a common understanding of
the information before moving into the board discussion.

3. Round One: What Does the Evidence Tell Us? (20–40 minutes)

Ashley asks each member:

"What is the most important finding from the ratings, building information, or
walkthroughs that should influence our decision, and why?"

Each member has approximately 2 minutes to respond.
Ashley calls on members individually, with no interruptions or debate.

4. Round Two: What Are the Tradeoffs? (40–60 minutes)

Ashley asks each member:

"What is the greatest educational, operational, financial, or community tradeoff we need
to consider when evaluating potential school closures?"

Each member has approximately 2 minutes.

The purpose is to understand different considerations rather than debate individual board
members. Members should avoid repeating points unless they are adding a different
perspective.

5. Round Three: Where Do We Go From Here? (60–80 minutes)

Ashley asks each member:

"Based on the information we have reviewed and the discussion we've had tonight, what
are your current thoughts about which school or schools, if any, should move forward for
public hearings on potential closure, and what is the most important reason for your
thinking?"

Each member has approximately 2 minutes.

This is not the time to introduce motions or take votes. It is an opportunity for each board
member to reflect on where they stand and explain their reasoning before the board reaches the
Action Items portion of the agenda.

While much of the board's discussion to date has focused on the potential closure of two
schools, that outcome is not a foregone conclusion. The board may ultimately decide to
consider the closure of one, two, or more schools, or none at all. Members should feel free to
express their current thinking without being limited to the configurations discussed previously.

Ashley will call on each member individually, without interruptions or debate. She will not tally
preferences or treat the discussion as a “straw poll.”

The purpose is to ensure every member has an opportunity to share their perspective before the
board considers any formal motions later in the meeting.

6. Conclude the Discussion (80–90 minutes)

Ashley and Cindy briefly summarize the discussion, including areas of agreement, differences in
judgment, and any significant questions that remain.

Ashley’s Closing Comments:

"Thank you for this discussion. I know these are really difficult discussions and decisions for our
board, administration, teachers, community members, and even our students. I appreciate
everyone's willingness to share their perspective and explain the considerations influencing their
thinking.

We have heard a range of views, and I recognize that this is not an easy decision for board
members who volunteered to serve.

Later in tonight's meeting, under Action Items, board members will have the opportunity to make
formal motions regarding whether specific schools should move forward for consideration
through the public hearing process.

I want to remind everyone that advancing a school for public hearings does not mean the
board has decided to close it. The purpose of those hearings is to provide additional
information and hear from our towns before any final decision is made. As we have reminded
community members at our listening sessions, we may be six towns but we are one
unified school district. Each of our schools belong to our entire HUUSD community."

The board will now proceed to Board Reports and the remaining discussion items on the
agenda.

This portion occurs after Board Reports and the policy first readings, consistent with the

"We have reached the Action Items portion of our agenda.

Earlier this evening, we discussed the information and considerations surrounding the potential

The question now before the board is whether we wish to formally consider the closure of any

Ashley states:
"I will ask that during this portion of the meeting that board members who wish to make a

Planning for the Public Hearings
Pietro Lynn emphasized that the public hearings should be structured intentionally to provide
meaningful opportunities for community input and to explain the reasons the district is
considering potential school closures. They are intended for all community members including
those who support a potential closure and those who oppose a potential closure.

He suggested that each hearing include:

   1.​ A brief presentation explaining the financial, enrollment, educational, and operational
       considerations that have led the board to examine school consolidation.
   2.​ Information about the proposed school closure or closures, including the anticipated
       implications for students, programs, and communities.
   3.​ An extended opportunity for public comment, with reasonable, consistently applied
       procedures.

Hearings held in affected communities may provide additional information specific to those
buildings.

No final closure action would be taken during these hearings.


January 6: Potential Final Closure Votes
Following the required public hearings and at least ten days' public notice stating the reasons for
any proposed closure, the board may consider final closure votes on January 6, 2027.
Under Article 6(E), an affirmative two-thirds vote of the Board of Directors is required to close a
school.

Separate motions for each proposed school closure would allow members to consider the public
testimony and evidence associated with each school individually.

Pietro Lynn or another attorney will be present at that meeting to provide guidance on
parliamentary and legal questions.


Voting Procedures
For the November 18 action items, Pietro confirmed that advancing a school for formal closure
consideration requires a majority of weighted votes cast.

For the January 6 final closure decisions, the Articles require a two-thirds approval threshold.

Given our 14-member board, a show of hands with individual votes recorded and weighted
totals calculated could be more efficient than calling on every member individually.




Final Reminder
The November 18 discussion and votes are about whether the board will formally
consider closing one or more schools and not whether those schools will actually close.

The public hearings are an essential part of the decision-making process. They provide
opportunities for the board to explain the circumstances, hear from residents throughout the
district, and consider additional information before making any final closure decisions.
