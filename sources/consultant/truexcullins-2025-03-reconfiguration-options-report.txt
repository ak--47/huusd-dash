Introduction
Harwood Unified Union School District (HUUSD) provides PreK to 12 grade education for the
students of Duxbury, Fayston, Moretown, Waitsfield, Warren and Waterbury Vermont. Due to
rising costs, the District is investigating long-term strategies to improve both educational
effectiveness and operational efficiency.
As a result, they have commissioned TruexCullins (TXC) to explore district reconfiguration
options that could include closing and repurposing schools to meet these goals.
For planning purposes, enrollment numbers were used from October 2024. Although the
district has not recently completed enrollment projections, the long-term trend is one of flat
to declining enrollment.
Thirteen planning options (other than status quo) were originally developed and presented to
the Building Use and Facilities Committee. Of these Thirteen, 6 were selected for cost
modeling. The next step is for the board to select up to 3 options for future study.
Future study includes 2 phases: Facility Evaluations and Concept Design. In the facility
evaluation phase, an architect-led team of engineers evaluates each building and site system

and makes recommendations for modernization. In Concept Design, site and building plans are
developed for each planning options. For both phases, a cost estimator is included to provide
cost estimates for each recommended improvement and planning option.
PROCESS
When exploring master planning options, it is essential to develop criteria by which to evaluate
each option. To do this, TXC created detailed questionnaires for educational leaders of each
school division: elementary, middle and high school. Their responses formed the basis for the
planning guidelines found in this report (see “Planning Guidelines” section of this report). Their
input provides the basis for understanding the educational effectiveness of each option.
As we stated earlier, however, educational effectiveness must be balanced with operational
efficiency. The good news is that these ideas are not always in tension. Some changes, such as
larger schools for example, can yield both results.
To evaluate the operational effectiveness of each option, TXC developed a cost model that
includes Deferred Maintenance/Capital Renewal, Ongoing Maintenance, New Construction and
Salary/Benefits for each option and extrapolates those costs over 30 years. This time frame was
used for cost savings analysis as it is the length of a bond, which is often a necessary
investment to achieve significant long term savings. A model was also developed for the
current configuration (Option 0), which provides a basis for evaluating potential cost savings or
additions for each option.
As you will see below, the cost models are very high-level and contain many assumptions. By
keeping the assumptions consistent across all options, it is the relative differences between the
options and the status quo that have significance.

                           COST MODEL
                           The components of the cost model are described below:
                           Net Replacement Value
                           Several of the components of the cost model are based on
                           APPA (Association of Physical Plant Administrators)
                           recommended guidelines which include Net Replacement
                           Value. For this study, this is calculated by multiplying the
                           total square footage of the facilities in each option by a unit
                           cost per sf (total project cost of $850/sf)

                           Deferred Maintenance/Capital Renewal
                           This term refers to the backlog of work required at each
                           facility to bring it up to a modern standard. Because
                           detailed facility evaluations have not been completed
                           (except for Harwood MSHS), this study uses the APPA
                           (Association of Physical Plant Administrators)
                           recommendation of 4% of net replacement value of the
                           HUUSD facilities. For this study (with the exception of
                           Option 0 - no bond), it is assumed that this work is funded
                           by a public bond vote.
                           Ongoing Maintenance
                           This is work required to keep facilities in good working
                           order. APPA recommends allotting 3% of net replacement
                           value for this purpose. As a side note, this level of budgeting
                           is rarely achieved by VT school districts. Because the 3%
                           includes maintenance salaries and these costs are included
                           in the Salaries/Benefits category, these costs are escalated at
                           a rate of 2% per year.
                           New Construction
                           Many of the planning options require additional space at
                           each school. This is calculated using a unit cost (cost per SF)
                           provided by a professional cost consultant. These costs are
                           escalated at a rate of 4.1% per year.
                           Salaries/Benefits
                           The cost of operating the district is the largest component
                           of the cost model. These figures are computed by the
                           HUUSD’s Director of Finance and Operations and are
                           derived from developing the staffing required for each
                           option and multiplying that number by an average cost per
                           employee for salaries and benefits. These costs are escalated
                           at a rate of 3.5% per year per HUUSD direction.

                          DETERMINING THE SCOPE OF NEW CONSTRUCTION
                          Other than Brookside Primary School, which is 4 sections per
                          grade, all of the Valley Schools are 1 section per grade. To
                          understand the extent of new construction needed for each
                          school in the Valley to grow, it was essential to establish the
                          minimum space requirements for a 2 section per grade school
                          or larger. The requirements for this calculation were gathered
                          from the response to the detailed questionnaires from the
                          building principals. By comparing the existing square footage
                          in the building to the minimum space requirements for a
                          larger building.
                          It is worth noting that as school size grows, there is a need for
                          certain types of space that are not critical for smaller schools.
                          A good example is a cafeteria, a space that becomes essential
                          when gymnasium or multi-purpose room utilization is high.
                          Another factor driving the minimum space requirements is
                          the stated goal of equitable access to learning, support and
                          co-curricular activities across the district. Applying these
                          standards to each school in each option ensures this goal
                          (from a facility standpoint).
                          BUILDING CAPACITY
                          For the purposes of this study, the capacity of each
                          elementary and middle school was calculated utilizing the
                          State Board of Education maximum of 20 students per
                          classroom for grades K-4 and 25 students per classroom for
                          grades 5 thru 8. The methodology was to divide the square
                          footage of each classroom (being used as a classroom) and
                          divide it by 50sf per student for Kindergarten and Middle
                          School science labs and 35sf per student for all other
                          classrooms. These amounts were compared with the state
                          maximums listed above and the smaller of the two numbers
                          was used.
                          Please note that these numbers are higher than HUUSD’s
                          optimal class size. There also may opportunities for more
                          efficient use of space within a building than the current usage.
                          Further study will required in the next phase of the master
                          planning process.
                          RECOMMENDED OPTIONS FOR FURTHER STUDY
                          Including the status quo, TXC developed 15 options for review
                          by the building committee. Due to the sheer number of
                          options, the committee narrowed them to 5 options for cost
                          modeling by HUUSD staff.

                           ALIGNMENT WITH STATE GOALS
                           Over the past several years, the State of Vermont (and others)
                           have been exploring resurrecting a school construction aid
                           program. In 2023, there was a Governor-appointed task force
                           which issued a comprehensive report. In 2024, Act 149
                           created a Legislative Working Group, whose task is to
                           propose the framework of a new program. More recently the
                           Governor has proposed a major overhaul of the educational
                           system, including governance, consolidation, and funding.
                           Act 149 included a (unfunded) grant program for school
                           facility master planning. It is widely anticipated that having a
                           district-wide facility master plan will be a prerequisite for
                           participating in a future school construction program. This
                           study, to the extent possible, is consistent with Act 149
                           guidelines and should help position HUUSD for eligibility in a
                           school construction program.
                           Many of planning initiatives in this report align with the
                           Governor’s educational transformation initiative currently
                           being discussed in the legislature such as creating larger
                           schools, larger class sizes and regional middle schools. In this
                           way, the planning options appear to be consistent with state
                           educational goals.

                           INTRODUCTION
                           As stated earlier, TXC provided detailed questionnaires to ele-
                           mentary, middle and high school educational leadership
                           teams (principals). These questionnaires asked both general
                           questions about school and class size, as well as specific
                           space needs for a potentially larger scale school. We then met
                           with each team to clarify and discuss their responses.
                           Essentially, these themes have served as the planning guide-
                           lines by which each planning options has been evaluated.
                           THEMES
                           The following themes emerged from their responses:
                           Equity
                           • Regardless of the number of schools, there should be eq-
                              uitable access to similar types of program offerings at
                              each school division (elementary, middle, high)

                           Travel Time
                           • Elementary School - 45 minute maximum bus ride
                           • Middle and High School - 60 minute maximum bus ride

                           Pre-School
                           • Centralized Pre-School
                           • 1 each for Waterbury-Duxbury and the Valley

                           Discussion: Both training and space needs are specialized.
                           Currently there is a pre-school in each elementary school. It is
                           also likely that full day PreK-4 could become a future require-
                           ment.
                            Elementary Schools
                           • K-4 or K-6
                           • 2 sections per grade minimum

                           Discussion: Currently all of the Valley elementary schools are 1
                           section per grade. Brookside is 4 sections per grade. Creating
                           larger schools has both operational and educational ad-
                           vantages. It allows for the hiring of full-time specials and sup-
                           port staff such as nurse, librarians, art and music. It also is
                           more efficient for support service staff (SPED, counseling, etc)
                           to serve a larger cohort of students.

                           From an educational perspective, larger schools allow for more
                           course and extracurricular offerings, as well an increase in col-
                           laborative opportunities between staff. There is also a social
                           benefit in having more peers to connect with.

                           Finally, small schools struggle with hiring qualified staff for
                           part-time positions, who much prefer full-time opportunities.
                           Middle Schools
                           • 1 for the Valley, 1 for Waterbury/Duxbury
                           • Consistent grade configuration between the 2 schools
                              (Grades 5-8, 6-8 or 7-8)
                           Discussion: The educators felt strongly that grade configura-
                           tions should be aligned between the two middle schools so
                           that students had equitable opportunities and were similarly
                           prepared for high school. They were not openly against a sin-
                           gle middle school for the District, but seemed to believe that
                           was not politically viable option.
                           High School
                           • Harwood Union High School should remain grades 9-12.
                           • There was some discussion of moving central office and
                              an alternative program (HCLC) back into the building.
                           • Adding a STEM lab and renovating the existing science
                              labs remains a focus for future improvements.
                           Alternative Programs
                           • Elementary School: 1 for Valley, 1 for Waterbury/Duxbury
                           • Middle School: 1 per District
                           • High School: 1 per District

                           Discussion: The alternative program would provide a thera-
                           peutic setting for students who cannot function adequately in
                           a classroom. Currently, there is a moratorium on new thera-
                           peutic school charters. Furthermore, it would be considerably
                           less expensive to the District to provide this support in-house.

                           GENERAL FINDINGS
                           Several themes have emerged from the cost modeling results:
                           •   Less square footage, and fewer facilities of greater ca-
                               pacity yields greater long-term savings.
                           Discussion: Reducing the number and overall square footage
                           results in fewer buildings of greater capacity, resulting in more
                           efficient space use, less building to maintain/heat and more
                           efficient staffing. Increasing school capacity is also consistent
                           with the educational benefits of creating larger (2 section per
                           grade per minimum) elementary schools
                           •   Achieving significant savings requires up-front invest-
                               ment in facilities.
                           Discussion: In addition to deferred maintenance, all of the fa-
                           cilities require additional classroom space to accommodate the
                           students identified in the options.
                           •   Achieving equity of space that supports programming
                               requires investment in facilities.
                           Discussion: In all options, additions or renovations were re-
                           quired to provide equitable access to support spaces and uni-
                           fied arts.
                           •   Bonding for projects saves substantial amounts vs
                               budgeting on a yearly basis for capital renewal/
                               deferred maintenance.
                           Discussion: In Option 0, costs for capital renewal/deferred
                           maintenance were calculated two ways: bonded and no bond.
                           The no-bond option costs $470m more over 30 years. This is
                           because bonding pays for the work in today’s dollars with fixed
                           or declining payment whereas budgeting for this work on a
                           yearly basis results in the cost of the work escalating at 4.1%
                           per year.
