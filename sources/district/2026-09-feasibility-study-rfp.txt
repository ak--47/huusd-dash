1. Invitation and Purpose
The Harwood Unified Union School District (HUUSD) invites proposals from qualified architectural firms or multidisciplinary consultant teams to conduct a comprehensive feasibility study and provide related architectural planning services concerning the potential consolidation of Fayston Elementary School, Moretown Elementary School, Waitsfield Elementary School, and Warren Elementary School into one central site serving the Mad River Valley at 193 Home Farm Way in Waitsfield. 
The study must provide the School Board and the public with objective, decision-ready information. It must evaluate whether a consolidated elementary facility is educationally, operationally, financially, environmentally, and practically feasible; compare it with reasonable alternatives; and identify the risks, benefits, costs, and implementation requirements of this option. The District has not made a final decision to consolidate, select a site, construct a facility, or close any school through issuance of this RFP.
The selected consultant will lead the feasibility phase. At the District’s sole option, and subject to authorization, funding, negotiation, and applicable procurement requirements, the engagement may be extended to additional architectural and engineering services for a preferred alternative. No future phase is guaranteed.
2. District and Project Background
HUUSD is a unified public school district serving the Vermont communities of Duxbury, Fayston, Moretown, Waitsfield, Warren, and Waterbury. This RFP focuses on the four PK–6 elementary schools serving Fayston, Moretown, Waitsfield, and Warren. The District seeks to understand whether a central elementary campus could strengthen educational opportunity and program delivery, improve operational and staffing efficiency, address long-term facility needs, and provide a sustainable use of public resources while recognizing the importance of local schools to their communities.
The feasibility study will specifically evaluate the potential development of a consolidated elementary school at 193 Home Farm Way, Waitsfield, Vermont.

3. Guiding Principles
* Student learning, belonging, safety, accessibility, and equitable access to programs must remain central to the analysis.
* The process must be transparent, fact-based, and understandable to residents across all affected communities.
* The study must objectively evaluate the feasibility of consolidation at 193 Home Farm Way and must not assume that consolidation is the appropriate outcome. 
* The analysis should compare the proposed consolidation with the reasonable baseline of continuing to operate the four existing elementary schools. 
* Recommendations must reflect Vermont’s rural context, climate, regulatory environment, transportation patterns, and evolving school-construction requirements.
* Concepts should emphasize durable, flexible, energy-efficient, low-carbon, and maintainable facilities that can adapt to enrollment and program change.
4. Scope of Services — Feasibility Study
Proposers may recommend refinements, but the proposal must address each task below and clearly identify exclusions, assumptions, and optional services.
4.1 Project Initiation and Data Validation
* Conduct a kickoff with the School Board’s designated committee and District leadership; confirm governance, decision points, communications, schedule, and data needs.
* Review available prior studies, facility assessments, enrollment data, educational plans, operating costs, transportation data, property records, and relevant HUUSD policies and Articles of Agreement.
* Review available information concerning 193 Home Farm Way, including property characteristics, existing, improvements, utilities, access, known environmental conditions and other available site information. 
* Document study assumptions, data limitations, evaluation criteria, and the process for updating material assumptions.
* Identify additional information or investigations necessary to complete the feasibility study. 
4.2 Educational Program and Enrollment
* Review the District’s most recent enrollment projections, which HUUSD updates annually and generally obtains from NESDEC (New England School Development Council). The District’s current enrollment projections will generally serve as the basis for enrollment planning and analysis for this study.
* Assess the reasonableness and applicability of the District’s enrollment projections for the purposes of this feasibility study, including identifying any significant assumptions, limitations, trends, or other considerations that may affect the analysis.
* Develop or validate, as appropriate, enrollment assumptions necessary to support the feasibility analysis, while clearly distinguishing any Consultant-developed assumptions or scenarios from the District’s current enrollment projections.
* Provide templates, guidance, and reasonable facilitation to support HUUSD staff in developing the educational program specifications for a potential consolidated PK–6 elementary school. HUUSD staff will lead and provide the substantive educational-program content and requirements. The Consultant shall provide reasonable coordination, facilitation, and documentation of this work within the base fee. Proposers shall not assume that a separate educational consultant is required unless specifically identified as an optional service.
* Evaluate projected enrollment in relation to potential grade configurations, program needs, school capacity, and the potential consolidation of Fayston, Moretown, Waitsfield, and Warren Elementary Schools.
* Develop or validate an educational program and space program for a consolidated PK–6 elementary school, including instructional spaces, specialized programs, administration, student support services, common areas, staff collaboration, circulation, storage, food service, physical education, arts, music, library/media, health, counseling, outdoor learning, after-school use community use and other program requirements identified through the study.
* Evaluate anticipated capacity, utilization, flexibility, adjacencies, and opportunities for future growth or changes in educational programming.
* Identify key educational opportunities, constraints, and tradeoffs associated with the potential consolidation, including those related to enrollment, grade configuration, program delivery, and school capacity.
4.3 Existing Facility and Property Assessment
* Review available condition information and conduct site visits sufficient to establish a comparable baseline for each existing school.
* Identify major building, code, accessibility, life-safety, envelope, structural, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, technology, security, site, water/wastewater, and deferred-maintenance issues affecting continued use or renovation.
* Estimate near-, mid-, and long-term capital needs under continued-operation alternatives. Clearly state where invasive investigation, testing, survey, or engineering analysis is excluded or recommended.
* Provide a high-level analysis of potential future disposition or adaptive reuse considerations for properties that could cease school use, while recognizing that detailed reuse planning and real-estate valuation are outside the base scope unless proposed as optional services.
4.4 Evaluation of central site at 193 Home Farm Way
The primary site-specific component of this RFP shall be an evaluation of the feasibility of developing a consolidated PK–6 elementary school at 193 Home Farm Way, Waitsfield, Vermont. 
The consultant shall:
* Review available property records, mapping, plans, surveys, and other information provided by the District.
* Conduct an appropriate site visit and field review.
* Evaluate the property's suitability for the proposed school use, including, available acreage and developable area, property boundaries and known easements, topography, access and circulation, traffic and transportation considerations, pedestrian and bicycle access, bus circulation and loading, parent drop-off and pick-up, emergency vehicle access, parking, utilities, water supply, wastewater/septic feasibility, stormwater management, drainage, soils, wetlands, flood hazards, environmental constraints, neighboring properties and uses, noise and other potential site impacts, outdoor learning and recreation opportunities, snow storage, expansion potential; and other conditions that could materially affect development.
* Identify known or reasonably foreseeable permitting and regulatory constraints.
* Determine whether the property appears capable of accommodating the anticipated school program and associated site requirements.
* Identify any portions of the property that may be unsuitable or impractical for development.
* Identify additional survey, geotechnical, environmental, traffic, wetlands, wastewater, utility, or other due diligence necessary before the District could proceed to design or property development.
* Develop a concept-level site plan demonstrating how the proposed school could reasonably be accommodated on the property.
* Identify major site-development costs and risks.
* Evaluate the potential for future expansion or adaptation of the site and facility.
* Prepare a fatal-flaw review for the subject site. Identify survey, geotechnical, environmental, title, or other due diligence required before site selection.
* Evaluate transportation, travel-time, and route implications associated with potential consolidation, including impacts on students and staff, rural road conditions, winter weather and road conditions, special transportation needs, bus fleet and driver requirements, parent/guardian traffic, arrival and dismissal patterns, and greenhouse-gas implications. The District and First Student will provide available transportation routing information, current routes, travel times, ridership, fleet, and related transportation analysis. The Consultant shall coordinate with HUUSD and First Student, review and incorporate the information and findings provided into the feasibility study, and identify any significant transportation-related considerations, impacts, or assumptions. Reasonable coordination, facilitation, and incorporation of transportation information shall be included in the Consultant's base fee.

4.6 Concept Development
* Prepare concept-level site plans and building test fits for the subject consolidation , sufficient to demonstrate capacity, circulation, parking, bus operations, outdoor learning/play, utilities, stormwater, and potential expansion.
* Prepare preliminary space programs and massing/area diagrams. Renderings are not required unless included as an optional service.
* Address construction delivery options, phasing, temporary facilities, schedule constraints, and continuity of educational operations.
4.7 Cost, Affordability, and Funding Analysis
* Prepare a concept-level capital cost estimate for development of a consolidated facility at 193 Home Farm Way.
* The estimate shall include, as applicable building construction, site development, utilities, permitting, testing, furnishings and equipment, technology, contingencies, design and engineering, escalation, owner costs, and other applicable soft costs.
* State the estimate class, basis, date, escalation assumptions, and expected accuracy range.
* Prepare comparable capital-cost estimates for reasonable alternatives.
* Develop 20- and 30-year life-cycle cost comparisons, including major renewal, energy, maintenance, staffing, transportation, and other material operating impacts. Show assumptions and provide sensitivity analysis for major cost drivers.
* Identify potentially applicable federal, State of Vermont, utility, energy, or other funding and financing opportunities. The study must align, to the extent practicable, with then-current Vermont Agency of Education guidance and statutory or regulatory requirements for school facilities planning and potential State school-construction aid, while clearly noting uncertainties and future approvals.
* Provide a preliminary affordability and implementation framework; do not characterize any funding source as committed unless formally documented by the funding authority.
4.8 Regulatory, Sustainability, and Resilience Review
* Identify likely local, State, and federal approvals and permitting pathways, including land use, environmental, stormwater, wastewater, accessibility, fire/life safety, and applicable school-facility standards.
* Address Vermont energy codes, high-performance building opportunities, electrification, renewable energy, indoor environmental quality, climate resilience, and practical low-carbon strategies.
* Identify material risks, decision dependencies, and investigations needed in subsequent phases. 


5. Minimum Qualifications 
HUUSD seeks proposals from qualified architectural firms or multidisciplinary consultant teams with demonstrated experience in public K–12 educational facilities planning, feasibility analysis, and related services. The proposer may be an architectural firm or a multidisciplinary team assembled to provide the full range of services necessary to complete the feasibility study.  
To be considered a responsible proposer, the firm or lead consultant must demonstrate the following minimum qualifications: 


5.1 Public K–12 Educational Facility Experience 
The firm or lead consultant must have a minimum of five (5) years of experience providing architectural, educational facility planning, feasibility study, master planning, facility assessment, or related professional services for public K–12 school districts or comparable public educational institutions. 


5.2 Elementary School Experience 
The firm or lead consultant must demonstrate substantial experience with the planning, evaluation, design, renovation, addition, replacement, or construction of public elementary school facilities.
5.3 Feasibility and Facilities Planning Experience
The firm or lead consultant must demonstrate experience conducting feasibility studies, facility assessments, master planning studies, educational programming studies, site evaluations, or comparable planning assignments for public school districts.
Experience should demonstrate the ability to evaluate multiple alternatives and provide objective recommendations based on educational, operational, facility, financial, site, regulatory, and community considerations.
5.4 Multi-School Planning and Consolidation Experience
The firm or lead consultant should demonstrate experience evaluating the consolidation, closure, relocation, replacement, or reconfiguration of multiple school facilities, or comparable multi-facility planning projects.
Experience involving the evaluation of multiple existing schools in relation to a potential consolidated facility is strongly preferred.
The District recognizes that firms may have extensive K–12 feasibility and facilities planning experience without having completed a school consolidation project. Accordingly, direct school consolidation experience is preferred but is not an absolute minimum qualification, and the District will consider comparable multi-facility planning experience.
5.5 Educational Programming and Enrollment Planning
The proposed team must demonstrate experience developing or evaluating educational programs, space requirements, enrollment projections, capacity, grade configurations, adjacencies, and other factors necessary to establish an appropriate program for a K–12 or elementary school facility.
5.6 Site Evaluation and Concept Planning
The proposed team must demonstrate experience evaluating potential school sites and developing concept-level site plans, building test fits, or comparable planning studies sufficient to determine whether a proposed site can reasonably accommodate the anticipated educational program and associated site requirements.
5.7 Cost Estimating and Life-Cycle Analysis
The proposed team must demonstrate experience preparing preliminary or concept-level construction cost estimates and evaluating capital and long-term operating costs associated with public educational facilities.
Experience with life-cycle cost analysis, capital planning, facility renewal, energy costs, maintenance costs, and other long-term financial considerations is preferred.
5.8 Public Engagement and School District Decision-Making
The proposed team must demonstrate experience working with school boards, district leadership, school staff, municipalities, community members, and other stakeholders in connection with educational facility planning, feasibility studies, capital projects, or comparable public decision-making processes.
5.9 Vermont and New England Experience
Experience with Vermont or northern New England public school facilities, including applicable regulatory, permitting, energy, accessibility, construction, public procurement, and school-facility planning requirements, is preferred.
Firms without Vermont experience may demonstrate comparable experience in other New England jurisdictions and shall describe their approach to becoming familiar with applicable Vermont requirements.
5.10 Proposed Project Team
The individuals identified in the proposal to perform the work must have demonstrated experience relevant to their assigned roles. The proposed team should collectively provide expertise in, as applicable:
* K–12 educational facility planning and architecture;
* Educational programming and enrollment analysis;
* Site planning and civil engineering;
* Transportation and traffic analysis;
* Construction cost estimating;
* Life-cycle cost and operating analysis;
* Sustainability and energy efficiency;
* Regulatory and permitting requirements; and
* Public and community engagement.
The proposer shall identify any anticipated subconsultants and describe their qualifications and role in the project.
5.11 Comparable Project References
The proposer must identify at least three (3) comparable public-school or public-facility projects completed or substantially completed within the past ten (10) years. The projects should demonstrate experience relevant to the services requested under this RFP.
For each project, the proposer shall identify:
* Client and location;
* Project name and year completed;
* Description and scope of services;
* Project size and/or enrollment served, where applicable;
* Firm's role and key team members;
* Study or project fee, where applicable;
* Resulting recommendation or outcome; and
* Client reference, including contact information.
At least one reference should demonstrate experience with a feasibility study, facilities master plan, consolidation analysis, site selection study, or comparable multi-facility planning assignment.
5.12 Responsibility Determination
Meeting the minimum qualifications does not guarantee selection or award of a contract. The District will evaluate the qualifications, experience, proposed team, approach, references, and other information submitted to determine whether each proposer is responsible and capable of satisfactorily performing the required services.
The District reserves the right to verify qualifications and references and to request additional information reasonably necessary to make a responsibility determination.

10. Evaluation and Selection
A District evaluation team will review all proposals received by the deadline to determine whether each proposal is responsive and conforming to the requirements of this RFP and whether the proposer is a responsible bidder capable of satisfactorily performing the required services.
The District may request clarification of a proposal, verify references, review the qualifications and experience of the proposed team, conduct interviews, and evaluate the proposer's ability to provide satisfactory service. The District may also evaluate the proposed schedule, understanding of the project, proposed approach, competency, and other factors relevant to determining whether a proposer is responsible and whether the proposal conforms to the RFP requirements.
Minimum Evaluation Factors
In determining whether a proposal is responsive and whether a proposer is responsible, the District may consider:
* Understanding of HUUSD's objectives, project requirements, and key risks;
* Quality and completeness of the proposed approach and work plan;
* Relevant educational-facility, feasibility-study, and consolidation experience;
* Qualifications, competency, availability, and continuity of the proposed project team;
* Experience with Vermont school facilities, permitting, regulatory requirements, and public processes;
* Ability to meet the required project schedule;
* References and demonstrated ability to provide satisfactory service;
* Compliance with all mandatory requirements, specifications, and submission requirements of this RFP; and
* Any other information reasonably necessary to determine the proposer's responsibility and ability to perform the contract.
Award
Following the evaluation described above, the District will identify proposals that are responsive, conforming to the specifications, and submitted by responsible proposers.
Consistent with applicable Vermont law, the District intends to award the contract from among the three or fewer lowest responsible bids conforming to the specifications, giving consideration to the purpose for which the services are required, the time required for performance, the competency and responsibility of the proposer, and the proposer's ability to render satisfactory service.
The District will not award a contract solely on the basis of the highest technical score or lowest price without first determining that the proposal conforms to the requirements of the RFP and that the proposer is responsible and capable of satisfactorily performing the required services.
The District reserves the right to request clarification, conduct interviews, verify references, reject any proposal that is nonresponsive or submitted by a proposer determined not to be responsible, and reject any or all proposals as permitted by law.
The District may negotiate final scope, schedule, staffing, and other contract terms with the proposer selected for award, provided that such negotiations do not materially alter the requirements of the RFP or otherwise conflict with applicable procurement requirements.
The District is not obligated to select a proposal that fails to conform to the RFP requirements or a proposer that the District determines is not responsible.
If the District determines that no proposal provides the required level of competency, responsibility, or ability to satisfactorily perform the services, the District reserves the right to reject all proposals and take such further action as permitted by law.
