   4. Benefits of Consolidation: 
Can we start to list some of the tangible benefits of consolidation? Has any of the work/studies we’ve done started to look at how consolidation would benefit the students/communities? Not just theoretical, but based in $$ and education benefits for our children. (Caitlin Lovegrove)


   * A great place to start is to review this policy paper posted by the Vermont Superintendents Association. It cites numerous studies that looked into optimal class sizing and finds that the research suggests that:


   * very small schools and classes can limit instructional flexibility, restrict opportunities for varied student groupings, and require unstable multi-age classroom configurations. 


   * Maintaining unusually small classes can also divert resources from interventions, professional development, and other programs that may have a greater effect on student achievement. 


   * ~400-student schools can better balance educational quality with the efficient use of staffing and facilities, while recognizing that transportation, infrastructure, and local student needs must be considered when evaluating consolidation.

   10. Closing one Valley School instead of two: 
What would closing just one Valley School look like for next year? If imperfect, would it at least give us more time to have a broader community discussion about these decisions? (Caitlin L, via community member)


   * From the models we have seen so far, this does not look like it would save enough money to make it worthwhile. The money spent on each school would not be a 1-1 match with the money saved if it were closed, given that some staff would be moved to a new location. We saw this the TruEx models (see slide 23 in the attached, based on this doc.) We also saw, on slide 21 of the Oct 14 meeting deck, that closing 2 schools in the Valley only saves $2.1M, so we could assume that closing one would save less than that.
