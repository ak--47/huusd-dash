# Building Use and Visioning Committee Minutes

Harwood Unified Union School District

October 12, 2026  \|  Via Zoom  \|  DRAFT Minutes

# **Call to Order and Attendance**

Chair J.B. Weir called the meeting to order at 1:36 p.m.

Board members present: J.B. Weir, Langford Davidson, Pam Eaton, Theo Hanna, Ashley Woods, Cindy Senning, Emily Dolloff, and Steve Rosenberg.

Administration and staff present: Superintendent Dr. Mike Leichliter, Chief Financial Officer Lisa Estler, and Ray Daigle, Buildings and Grounds. Curtis Siegmann provided technical support. Members of the public attended remotely, including Sarah Nagle.

# **Public Comment**

Sarah Nagle expressed appreciation for the committee’s work and asked that consolidation options keeping Moretown Elementary School open be considered. She described her family’s positive experience with the school and noted that the earlier planning options did not retain Moretown.

# **Approval of September 1 Minutes**

Langford Davidson moved to approve the September 1, 2026 minutes with an amendment correcting the superintendent’s name from “Dr. Michael Mike” to Dr. Michael Leichliter. Ashley Woods seconded. The motion passed with the amendment; no numerical vote tally was stated.

# **Designation of Minutes Recorder**

Dr. Leichliter volunteered to record the minutes, and Chair Weir accepted.

# **Committee Appointments**

Theo Hanna expressed interest in joining the committee and clarified the spelling of her last name. Chair Weir also reported Macon Phillips’s interest in joining. The proposed appointments of Hanna and Phillips, along with Langford Davidson’s proposed appointment as co-chair, were deferred to the October 14 full-board meeting. Dr. Leichliter explained that the board chair makes committee appointments subject to full-board affirmation. No appointment vote was taken at this meeting.

# **Review of Planning and Facility Reports**

Chair Weir led a review of the December 2024 planning update, March 2025 district reconfiguration options, and facility evaluation materials linked in the agenda. The discussion focused on the development of the earlier options and whether changing enrollment and financial conditions warranted revisiting them.

Dr. Mike explained that TruexCullins worked with administrators and the board to examine building capacity, possible grade configurations, transportation, and facility needs. Enrollment has declined more than initially anticipated. Geographic distances and terrain, teacher licensing, and instructional needs affect the feasibility of different configurations. Chair Weir suggested another discussion with school administrators as planning continues.

Members discussed the importance of each school to its community. The earlier reports primarily addressed logistical and facility considerations and did not fully capture the community value of individual schools.

# **Facility Conditions and Environmental Considerations**

Dr. Mike and Ray Daigle reviewed the challenges of renovating Brookside, originally built as a 19th century high school. These include space for specialists and student services, accessibility requirements, ventilation, roof needs, an undersized cafeteria, and aging brickwork. Crossett Brook’s more recent construction was identified as a factor in its suitability for potential reconfiguration.

In response to questions about PCBs, Dr. Mike reported that Warren had been tested and the identified source in the mechanical room had been remediated. Daigle described additional remediation during the 2017 renovation and explained that discovery of PCBs requires a mitigation plan. Administration reported that state testing had halted because funding was exhausted and that environmental conditions would need evaluation during future renovations.

Administration confirmed that flood-related improvements at Moretown and Crossett Brook were completed before the facility assessment and were reflected in its estimates. Elementary enrollment was reported as declining except at Moretown; Harwood enrollment was described as approximately stable.

# **Educational Priorities and Financial Context**

Members emphasized that future presentations and public communications should explain the educational benefits and effects of consolidation, including access to programming, student services, and peer interaction. Pam Eaton and Theo Hanna described families’ attachment to Brookside alongside their concern for educational opportunities and the district’s long-term financial stability. Cindy Senning recommended explicitly identifying educational advantages in comparisons of options.

Steve Rosenberg cautioned that consolidation should not be presented as resolving all financial challenges posed by the state’s funding policies. Members discussed the need to consider educational quality, declining enrollment, operating costs, and deferred maintenance together.

# **Consolidation Options and Cabot Update**

Chair Weir discussed how the Cabot concept relates to the earlier single-Valley-school option and described limits on expanding the existing Waitsfield school. Cabot proposals are due October 30\. The committee anticipated reviewing them at its next meeting; no proposal or site was selected.

Chair Weir raised a possible alternative retaining Moretown while combining the other three Valley elementary schools at another location. Members discussed possible capital savings, continuing maintenance costs, and whether additional modeling would duplicate earlier work. Several members favored reviewing the forthcoming financial models and Cabot proposals before commissioning additional scenarios.

Theo Hanna asked that Moretown remain a possibility within a two-Valley-school configuration. Ashley Woods confirmed that no individual school had been ruled out and that a two-school option remained under consideration. No consolidation configuration was adopted.

The discussion also addressed expansion capacity, transportation distances, and attendance areas based on geography and efficiency rather than town boundaries. Daigle clarified that Moretown School owns only a few acres, with the larger adjoining property owned by the town, and that Warren School occupies town land under an easement. Potential use of adjoining town property would require further consideration.

# **Financial Modeling and Next Steps**

Lisa Estler reported that the administration would present preliminary models at the October 14 board meeting addressing the board’s three charges:

* Reductions needed to remain below the FY28 excess-spending threshold.  
* Consolidating four Valley elementary schools into two.  
* Merging the district’s middle schools.

The models include short-term and long-term considerations. Estler explained that the elementary model does not select specific schools. Board direction would guide more detailed analysis. The presentation was expected to be linked to the board agenda late Wednesday afternoon, approximately 4:00 p.m.

Dr. Mike reminded members of the October 14 workshop beginning at 5:00 p.m. on the overall budget process. Assistance with email two-factor authentication would also be available.

The committee’s next discussion will include the Cabot proposals and the financial modeling presented to the full board. Proposed committee appointments will be brought to the October 14 board meeting.

# **Next Meeting**

The next committee meeting was scheduled for Monday, November 9, 2026, at 1:30 p.m. Chair Weir asked that the meeting be kept to approximately one hour if possible.

Langford Davidson moved to adjourn. Ashley Woods seconded. The meeting adjourned at 2:45 p.m.
