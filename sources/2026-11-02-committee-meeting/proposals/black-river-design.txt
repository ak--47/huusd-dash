PROJECT UNDERSTANDING
Based on many factors, HUUSD is considering consolidating four elementary schools. With the opportunity
to purchase the 193 Home Farm Way property in Waitsfield, it is now greatly important that the district
evaluate the possibilities, advantages, drawbacks, and challenges of all sides of this decision before
moving forward, or passing on this opportunity..

The integrated team of Black River Design Architects, SE Group planners and designers, DuBois & King
engineers, TCE/Bowman, and Merkur Construction brings complementary expertise in working with
Vermont schools to assist in facilities scoping and planning-level cost estimation to provide the required
level of detail to support facility assessment and decision making.

Rather than advancing the proposed outcome, our role is to help HUUSD understand its options, clarify
tradeoffs, and develop a common framework for decision-making. At the end of this process, the District
will have the information needed to evaluate the key opportunities now available around the configuration
of the four Valley schools.


APPROACH & WORK PLAN
1. Project Initiation and Data Validation

After a kickoff meeting with the committee, we will review all data provided by the district, including prior
studies and facility assessments, operating cost data, transportation data, enrollment data, etc. Parallel to
District data review, we will review data on 193 Home Farm Way. We will document any limitations or
assumptions in the provided data, and note any further investigations needed.

Deliverable: Summary of data findings.


2. Educational Program and Enrollment

Our team will review HUUSD's enrollment projections and consider the implications of these projections on
the reconfiguration options. We will consider the basis of the projections, the likely margin of error, and
evaluate the potential impact of this on district facility needs.

The program of space needs that will support the educational program for a combined Pre-K through sixth
grade school should be developed with input from all four existing schools. We will coordinate this effort,
working with staff to evaluate current space usage and needs, typical requirements, and state standards to
create a program of space needs to reinforce educational goals while taking advantage of any
opportunities that consolidation gives for efficiency. We understand the importance of giving special
consideration to exterior space use.

Deliverable: Consolidated school program of space needs document.
3. Existing Facility and Property Assessment

In addition to reviewing prior studies and facility assessments, we will visit each of the four existing schools
to confirm or adjust previous data.

Based on past expense data and these evaluations, we can estimate ongoing capital needs for these
schools if they are to remain operating for the district. In the case of consolidation, we can provide a high-
level analysis of potential uses for each school based on its individual location, site opportunities, access,
adjacencies and connections, and building configuration.

Deliverables: Initial facilities assessment report on each of the four schools, Materials to support a public
meeting summarizing existing conditions of facilities.



4. Evaluation of 193 Home Farm Way

BRD and our consultant team will evaluate the 193 Home Farm Way building, property, and surrounding
potential opportunities. TCE/Bowman has been involved with design and permitting at Mad River Park and
understands its opportunities and limitations. The evaluation of 193 Home Farm Way will include the
following:
    • Existing physical conditions: grading, drainage, access, etc.
     • Existing regulatory conditions: zoning and land use permitting requirements, easements, etc.
    • Identify particular site development costs, risks, and areas where further investigation or testing is
        recommended (i.e. hazardous materials, geotechnical, etc.)
     • Evaluate transportation changes from existing configuration to a consolidated configuration,
        including bus travel times. A full traffic study is offered as an additional service in this proposal.
     • Program fit: Does the site work for the program desired by the district
     • Existing building size and compatibility with program needs
    • Potential for renovation
    • Potential for additions
    • Potential for future site expansion
    • Site needs - parking, playgrounds, drop-off, pedestrian access
    • Code compliance
    • This evaluation will include a schematic plan for the building and site to help determine fit. It may
        include evaluation of partial consolidation options as well as full consolidation of all four schools.

Deliverable: Initial facility assessment report on Home Farm Way property, schematic "fit" plan.



5. Concept Development

Based on all information acquired and developed in Phases 1-4, we will develop up to three (3) schematic
plan options (additional options can be provided as additional services). These options will include building
plan layout to meet the program of space needs developed in Phase 2, and site layout for bus and car drop-
off, parking, playgrounds, stormwater management, pedestrian circulation, connection to adjacent sites
(sports fields and other public recreation facilities), outdoor classrooms, utilities, and future expansion.

Conceptual design will also address arrival and dismissal patterns, and greenhouse-gas implications /
potential for longer idling times due to consolidation.

Construction delivery options, phasing and schedule, along with impact on educational operations, will also
be evaluated.

Deliverables: Up to three (3) schematic consolidation plan options plus materials to communicate the
concepts in a public presentation.



6. Cost, Affordability, and Funding Analysis

BRD and our cost estimator will provide concept-level cost estimates for each of the development
concepts at 193 Home Farm Way. These estimates will include building construction, sitework and utilities,
permitting, testing, FF&E, contingencies, design and engineering fees, OPM fees and other owner costs,
and factors for inflation.

Long-term life-cycle cost comparisons will be developed based on owner-provided data about current
energy, maintenance, and material upgrade costs for the four existing buildings and assumptions for cost
of energy and maintenance for a renovated/new structure at Home Farm Way. Our team can help you
extrapolate cost impacts on staffing and transportation resulting from potentialconsolidation.

Potential funding and financing opportunities will be identified. Ongoing coordination with Vermont AOE
guidance and requirements for future state school construction aid will be needed for the duration of this
study.

Deliverable: Concept-level cost estimate for Home Farm Way construction / modification, up to 3 options.



7. Regulatory, Sustainability, and Resilience Review

The team will do a high-level review of up to three (3) conceptual design options for code compliance,
including Life Safety, IBC, ADA, Vermont Commercial Building Energy Standard (CBES), stormwater,
wastewater, and other applicable standards.

We will identify primary opportunities toward sustainability, resilience, and indoor environmental quality,
including carbon impact. At this stage this investigation would focus mainly on the existing structure in
terms of systems and envelope improvement opportunities, but could involve consideration of net-zero
goals, on-site solar, biomass delivery access, or geothermal well location analysis.
Deliverable: Full report, including all task reports compiled and coordinated, and materials for public
communication of the project developed.

8. Engagement, Meetings, and Final Report

The team will compile materials for the District to communicate key project process and information to the
public, including:
   -   Summary of existing facilities conditions for each of the four schools (Phase 3)
   -   Schematic consolidation plan options (Phase 5)
   -   Project takeaways and final documents (Phase 8)

Deliverable: Materials for public communication of the project process and key findings. Full report and
project summary, including all compiled and coordinated data, exhibits, and reports.



SUGGESTED OPTIONAL SCOPES OF SERVICE
We have included the services that we feel are necessary to complete the requested scope of work. There
are some optional services that may want to be considered to provide a more complete picture of the
impact of potential solutions to consolidation in the four Valley towns.

   •   Existing School Facilities Civil Engineering Analysis – additional $8,000
   •   Existing School Facilities Structural Analysis – additional $7,000
   •   Additional Public Process and Extended Engagement Services – additional $12,000

Additional Optional Services:

   •   Traffic study at the 193 Home Farm Way property. With the potential increased occupancy comes
       increased traffic. Knowing if consolidation will have a significant adverse impact on traffic flow in
       the area could be useful information. We have not included this in our base services, but
       TCE/Bowman can provide a comprehensive traffic study that should be desired for an additional fee
       of $10,000.
   •   PCB testing at all facilities. PCBs have become a significant concern in many Vermont Schools.
       Burlington High School is an example that has been in the news, but it is a concern at many of the
       schools in the State that were built before the mid 1980s. Although having this information is
       informative, it can also be very disruptive if PCB levels are found to be high in any of your existing
       facilities.
   •   Building or site renderings. We have not included any building or site renderings in our base
       services. Basic plan diagrams to determine approximate configuration and capacity are included
       but are not planned to be to a fully rendered level of detail.

Condi•on, Func•on, and Fit
Black River Design, Architects has spent nearly ﬁve decades helping Vermont school districts make defensible decisions
about their facili•es. Our work begins with building condi•on assessments and func•onal reviews that catalogue
physical deﬁciencies and evaluate how well each building serves the educa•onal programs it is meant to support. That
dis•nc•on ma•ers. A building can be structurally sound and s•ll fail to meet the needs of the community it serves,
and the reverse is equally true. Understanding both dimensions is the founda•on of useful facility planning.

Defensible Solu•ons
From that founda•on, we help districts work through the harder ques•ons: whether to renovate, replace, or
consolidate; how to sequence improvements across mul•ple buildings; and how to equitably align long-term capital
investment with educa•onal goals and ﬁscal reality. Sound facility planning depends on cost informa•on that is both
accurate and meaningful to the people who will ul•mately vote on the outcome. We integrate reliable cost es•mates
throughout the decision-making process so that stakeholders can compare op•ons. When the analysis is thorough, the
costs are transparent, and the methodology is consistent, recommenda•ons are easier to defend and decisions are
more likely to hold.

Trusted Partnerships
BRD has built long-standing rela•onships with some of Vermont’s most complex mul•-school, mul•-community
districts. That experience has directly shaped our approach to facility and func•onal analysis. Since 1995 we have
worked with Lamoille South Supervisory Union across all six of their buildings. Our work with Washington Central
Supervisory Union produced a living document allowing the board to assess and sequence improvements across
the en•re district, spanning code compliance, energy eﬃciency, deferred maintenance, and safety. We have also
partnered with Slate Valley Uniﬁed Union School District on mul•-community facility planning. These engagements
have reinforced that func•onal analysis in a mul•-building district is not simply a building-by-building exercise. It
requires a system-level view of how facili•es collec•vely serve or fail to serve the district’s educa•onal mission, and a
framework for making priori•es legible across communi•es with diﬀerent needs and perspec•ves. MMUUSD presents
exactly this kind of complexity: ﬁve towns, each with a dis•nct iden•ty and rela•onship to its school, asked to make
decisions together that will aﬀect each community diﬀerently. We understand that dynamic and know how to work
within it produc•vely.
