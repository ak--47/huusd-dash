Project Understanding
We understand that the Harwood Unified Union School District (HUUSD) is evaluating whether four PK–6 elementary schools—Warren, Fayston,
Waitsfield, and Moretown—could be consolidated at 193 Home Farm Way in Waitsfield, the former Cabot Creamery headquarters. The central
question is not simply whether the site can accommodate a consolidated school, but whether consolidation offers a feasible, educationally
sound, and financially sustainable alternative to maintaining and improving the four existing schools and other reasonable alternatives.

This decision requires balancing potential operational and financial efficiencies against the educational, social, and community value of local
schools. The rural context of the Mad River Valley adds complexity: consolidation could change student travel times, bus routes, transportation
costs, and family routines differently across the four communities. Winter road conditions, severe weather, route reliability, and emergency
access must also be considered alongside facility needs, educational programming, and long-term costs.

The proposed site presents its own feasibility questions. The study must determine whether the former headquarters can accommodate
HUUSD’s educational program and projected enrollment, what renovations or additions may be required, and whether site conditions,
infrastructure, environmental constraints, or permitting requirements could affect cost and schedule. Equally important is establishing the
investment needed to maintain and improve the existing schools so that alternatives can be compared fairly.

We recognize that school facilities are closely tied to community identity and local relationships, and that the benefits and burdens of
consolidation may be experienced differently across the four communities. Our collaborative, transparent approach will incorporate stakeholder
perspectives while grounding findings in validated data, consistent evaluation criteria, and clearly documented assumptions.

Key Risks and Information Needs
Our feasibility analysis will focus on five interconnected areas:

     •    Site and Building Feasibility: Educational capacity, building reuse, site circulation, infrastructure, environmental constraints, and
          potential fatal flaws.

     •    Educational Needs and Enrollment: Current and projected enrollment, program requirements, student services, and facility
          functionality under each alternative.

     •    Transportation and Equitable Access: Student travel times, bus operations, winter travel reliability, costs, and impacts on families
          across the four communities.

     •    Capital and Long-Term Costs: Comparative construction, renovation, facility renewal, operating, maintenance, staffing, and
          transportation costs, along with realistic funding assumptions.

     •    Implementation and Community Impacts: Required investigations, regulatory approvals, funding decisions, public processes,
          schedule considerations, and community perspectives.

We will work with HUUSD to validate available information, identify gaps, and determine where additional investigation is needed. The study
will provide an objective comparison of consolidation at 193 Home Farm Way against continued operation of the existing schools and other
reasonable alternatives. Our goal is to give HUUSD and its communities a clear understanding of the feasibility, trade-offs, risks, and next steps
associated with each option—without presuming the outcome.

An objective feasibility determination will require current enrollment projections, educational program requirements, facility-condition and
capital-needs assessments, site and property information, transportation data, operating costs, comparable cost estimates, and applicable
regulatory and funding information. We will assess the reliability of available information, document assumptions and limitations, and identify
additional surveys, testing, or specialized investigations needed to resolve material uncertainties.

The study must establish whether consolidation is a viable option—not simply demonstrate how to build a school on the proposed site. A
credible recommendation will depend on a fair comparison of reasonable alternatives, validated information, transparent assumptions, and a
clear understanding of the risks, tradeoffs, and unresolved questions associated with each path forward.

Approach and Work Plan
         Development of a Feasibility Study for Consolidation of Four Elementary Schools at 193 Home Farm Way,
         Waitsfield, Vermont

         Our goal is to provide Harwood Unified Union School District (HUUSD) and the communities it serves with an objective, data-
         driven roadmap for making informed decisions about elementary school consolidation and long-term facility needs. We will
         evaluate the feasibility of consolidating four elementary schools at 193 Home Farm Way in Waitsfield alongside maintaining
         and improving the existing schools and other reasonable alternatives.

         Through careful analysis and concept development, we will identify options that address HUUSD’s educational program,
         enrollment, operational, and facility needs, both now and into the future. These options will be evaluated through a
         collaborative, transparent, and interactive process that incorporates stakeholder and community input. The study will
         culminate in a comprehensive feasibility assessment, with comparative findings, recommendations, and an implementation
         roadmap to guide the District’s next steps.

         Our approach will be tailored to HUUSD’s values, vision, and goals, drawing on our experience with facility assessments, school
         planning, and consolidation feasibility studies. The work plan is organized around eight key components:

         1. Project Kick Off and Data Validation
         2. Program Development and Enrollment Analysis
         3. Existing Facilities Assessment
         4. Site Screening and Evaluation
         5. Concept Design and Alternatives
         6. Cost, Life Cycle, Funding


         Part 1 – Kick off Meeting / Data Validation
         Goals
         Establish a Shared Foundation for the Feasibility Study. Align the project team and HUUSD leadership on project goals,
         priorities, decision-making criteria, and expectations. Validate available data and documentation, identify information gaps, and
         confirm a coordinated work plan to support a thorough, objective evaluation of consolidation and alternative scenarios.


         Tasks
         • Conduct Project Kickoff: Meet with District leadership and key representatives to confirm project objectives, priorities,
           scope, roles, responsibilities, communication protocols, and decision-making processes.
         • Confirm Goals and Evaluation Criteria: Establish shared criteria for evaluating consolidation at 193 Home Farm Way against
           maintaining the existing schools and other viable alternatives.
         • Review Available Documentation: Inventory and review existing facility assessments, building and site plans, enrollment
           projections, educational program information, capital improvement plans, operating costs, and other relevant District
           records.
         • Validate Key Data and Assumptions: Work with HUUSD to confirm the accuracy, relevance, and completeness of available
           information, including enrollment, building capacity, facility conditions, operating costs, and program requirements.
         • Identify Information Gaps: Document missing, outdated, or conflicting information and identify additional documentation,
           site investigations, or analysis needed to support the feasibility assessment.
         • Confirm Stakeholders and Engagement: Identify key District and community stakeholders, establish engagement
           expectations, and coordinate opportunities for input throughout the study.
         • Finalize Work Plan and Schedule: Confirm project milestones, deliverables, responsibilities, and decision points to guide the
           study and maintain alignment between the project team and HUUSD.

          Part 3 – Existing Facilities Assessment
          Complete a comprehensive facilities condition assessment with detailed findings for each school and site

          Goals
          Evaluate Existing Facility Conditions and Long-Term Needs. Assess the physical condition, educational functionality, and

             improvement plans, and other relevant documentation to understand existing conditions and known deficiencies.
          • Assess Building Conditions: Evaluate major building systems, including structural, building envelope, roofing, mechanical,
             electrical, plumbing, and fire protection systems, identifying deficiencies, deferred maintenance, and anticipated capital
             renewal needs.

             enrollment, accessibility, student services, and school operations. Identify space constraints, functional limitations, and
             opportunities for improvement.

             life safety, and accessibility, noting items requiring further investigation.
          • Assess Energy Performance and Sustainability: Review available energy-use information and building system performance
             to identify opportunities for improved efficiency, electrification, and reduced operating costs.
          • Identify Near- and Long-Term Capital Needs: Summarize priority repairs, system replacements, renovations, and other
             anticipated investments needed to maintain and improve the existing schools.
          • Develop a Comparative Baseline: Document facility conditions, functional needs, and anticipated capital investments to
             inform the comparison of maintaining the existing schools against consolidation and other alternatives.

          Part 5- Concept Design and Alternative
          Goal
          Test Feasibility Through Comparative Design Alternatives. Develop and evaluate conceptual design alternatives to determine
          how well 193 Home Farm Way can accommodate HUUSD’s educational program, enrollment needs, and operational
          requirements. Use comparative concepts to illustrate the opportunities, constraints, and trade-offs of consolidation relative to
          maintaining the existing schools and other viable alternatives, supporting an objective, informed decision.


          Task
          • Establish Design Criteria: Confirm educational program requirements, enrollment assumptions, space needs, operational
            priorities, and site constraints to guide concept development.
          • Develop Conceptual Alternatives: Prepare planning-level concepts that explore how the proposed site and existing building
            could accommodate the District’s needs, including adaptive reuse, renovation, additions, or new construction as appropriate.
          • Evaluate Site and Building Fit: Assess site circulation, arrival and dismissal, bus and parent drop-off, parking, outdoor learning
            and play areas, accessibility, building organization, and opportunities to support effective school operations.
          • Compare Alternatives: Evaluate each concept against consistent criteria, including educational suitability, building and site
            feasibility, capacity, flexibility, sustainability, cost, schedule, and implementation risks.
          • Illustrate Opportunities and Trade-Offs: Prepare conceptual plans and supporting graphics that communicate the scale,
            organization, and implications of each alternative, identifying key benefits, limitations, and outstanding questions.
          • Refine Findings and Recommendations: Use the comparative analysis to identify feasible options, highlight additional
            investigation needed, and inform the Feasibility Study’s final recommendations.

          Product / Deliverable
          • Concept-level educational space programs, site plans, test fits, and diagrams for 193 Home Farm Way. Transportation and
            circulation analysis.
