Before we begin public comment this evening, I want to address concerns raised about the public comment process at our October 28 meeting.

First, I want to acknowledge the extraordinary level of community participation at that meeting. We recognize that many people made arrangements to attend, prepared remarks, and came hoping to share their perspectives. Some were unable to speak, and we understand the frustration that caused. We also recognize that we could have communicated the process for signing up to speak more clearly.

Following the meeting, the Board received a written complaint alleging a violation of Vermont's Open Meeting Law by limited public comment. Board leadership consulted with the attorney who represents the School Board to review the concerns and our obligations under the law.

Vermont's Open Meeting Law, specifically 1 V.S.A. § 312(h), requires that the public be given a "reasonable opportunity to express its opinion on matters considered by the public body during the meeting." The statute also states that "Public comment shall be subject to reasonable rules established by the chairperson."

The law does not prescribe a specific number of minutes for public comment. Rather, it provides discretion to the chair to establish reasonable rules while ensuring an opportunity for the public to be heard.

**After reviewing the circumstances and consulting with legal counsel, it is our position that the Board did not violate Vermont's Open Meeting Law.** The 15-minute public comment period was established in consideration of an agenda expected to take approximately four hours, the substantial information that needed to be presented, and the fact that no decisions on school closures or the FY28 budget were being made that evening.

**At the same time, complying with the law does not mean we cannot reflect on how to improve our process.** We recognize the significance of the issues before us and the strong desire of community members to be heard. Because we have a more limited agenda this evening, we will provide an extended opportunity for public comment, with up to three minutes for each speaker. We will also clearly explain how those attending in person or remotely can request to speak.

I also want to emphasize that we understand just how difficult the prospect of closing a community school is. Our schools are much more than buildings. They are central to the identity of our communities, and the possibility of closing one understandably brings strong emotions, concerns, and questions about the future.

**No decision has been made to close any school.** We are evaluating options in response to significant financial pressures as a result of Act 169, and these discussions remain part of that process.

It is also important for everyone to understand that our district's Articles of Agreement establish specific requirements should the Board decide to pursue a school closure.

Under Article 6(E), before the Board could vote to close a school, it must hold **at least three public hearings** regarding the proposed closure, including at least one hearing in the community where the school is located. If, after those hearings, the Board intends to vote on closing a school, it must provide public notice stating the reason for the proposed closure at least ten days before the vote. A school closure would then require an affirmative vote of two-thirds of the Board.

These are important protections built into the formation of our district. They ensure that communities directly affected by a proposed school closure have additional, formal opportunities to receive information, ask questions, and express their views before any final decision.

We are not at that point in the process, and I do not want anyone to interpret our current discussions as a decision that has already been made nor do I want to diminish the serious financial picture for the 2027-2028 school year.

We appreciate the extraordinary level of engagement from our communities. We know these are difficult conversations, and while we may not always agree, our responsibility is to approach them with openness, respect, and a commitment to listening.

With that, we will move to an extended public comment period.
