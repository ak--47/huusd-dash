Superintendent Report
TO:        HUUSD School Board
FROM:        Dr. Mike Leichliter
DATE:        October 28, 2026
RE:        Superintendent’s Report to the Board
As we approach the end of the first quarter, I am grateful for the people who make our schools places of learning and belonging. We have faced some difficult days, and we are fortunate to have outstanding students, teachers, and support staff who continue to look out for one another. Their daily efforts to meet students’ needs and create a positive school climate deserve our recognition and appreciation. This month’s report also includes our annual October 1 enrollment update, which helps guide our planning for the years ahead.

Our Harwood community is grieving the tragic death of a student, another heartbreaking loss following the death of a student last month. Our thoughts remain with the student’s family, friends, classmates, and all who are affected. I want to commend our staff, counselors, and principals for their hard work, compassion, and steady presence as they support students and one another through this difficult time. I also deeply appreciate the call I received at home this past Sunday from Secretary of Education Zoie Saunders, who also reached out to speak with me following last month’s tragedy. Secretary Saunders will attend this Thursday’s student assembly at Harwood to offer her support and convey to students and staff that people throughout Vermont are holding our school community in their thoughts.
Student Enrollment 2026–2027
October 1 is the annual enrollment benchmark used by both HUUSD and the state for year-to-year comparisons. Our October 1 K–12 enrollment is 1,567 students, including Early College participants, compared with 1,591 last October, a decrease of 24 students (1.5%). Although some figures have been adjusted since the preliminary September presentation, the overall trends remain unchanged.
The largest decreases continue to be in the Valley elementary schools, where combined K–6 enrollment is 414, down from 439 last October. Districtwide kindergarten enrollment is 98, compared with 109 last year. These patterns continue to inform our planning for staffing, programs, and facilities.
Class of 2026 Senior Survey
We recently received the Vermont Student Assistance Corporation’s (VSAC) Senior Survey results for Harwood’s Class of 2026, with 85.7% of the class participating. The results offer several encouraging highlights about students’ plans and preparation for life after high school. Among respondents, 76.7% planned to continue their education or enter a registered apprenticeship in the fall, compared with 71.0% statewide.
Harwood respondents also reported higher participation than their statewide peers in career fairs, college visits, and internships. Financial aid preparation was another strength: 73.3% reported that they or their parents had completed the FAFSA, compared with 60.0% statewide. In addition, approximately 72% agreed that their teachers cared about them as individuals, and 70% agreed that Harwood provided assistance to continue their education. These findings give us reasons to celebrate our graduates’ aspirations and recognize the teachers, school counselors, families, and community partners who support students as they plan their next steps.
Articles of Agreement and Board Voting Weights
During tonight’s meeting, I will review the district’s Articles of Agreement. One question I previously researched was when the Board last updated its voting weights following the 2020 census. The calculations were presented on October 13, 2021, and on October 27, 2021, the Board approved, without objection, the revised voting weights and a corresponding amendment to the Articles of Agreement. The voting weights per Board member are: Duxbury, 5.20%; Fayston, 5.00%; Moretown, 6.40%; Waitsfield, 6.70%; Warren, 7.20%; and Waterbury, 9.75%.
Additionally, the Articles of Agreement and the presentation reference the Waterbury-Duxbury merger documents. They can be found in this appendix towards the end.
Winooski Valley Cooperative Educational Service Area (CESA)
As the Board’s appointed representative to the Winooski Valley Cooperative Educational Service Area (CESA), I am required by 16 V.S.A. § 603(c) to provide updates “on a quarterly basis to the member’s appointing supervisory union board at an open board meeting.” I also serve as chair, and we last met on Monday, October 26. Our work remains focused on establishing the organization and identifying how shared services could benefit our districts. We elected Libby Bonesteel from Montpelier Roxbury School District as treasurer, discussed financial arrangements, and returned the draft bylaws to the committee that created them for further revisions before considering adoption. Members emphasized the importance of understanding the services and benefits before making financial commitments. Additionally, I will be working with two fellow CESA board members to develop a process for an assessment of regional needs and existing strengths to help guide our discussion at the January meeting. 
Information on members, meetings, agenda, minutes, and other information can be found on the Barre Unified Union School District website who has agreed to host our official website for the time. Our next meeting will be held on January 25, 2026, at 4:30 p.m., at Montpelier High School.
