Board authority to adjust attendance boundaries
Article 12, p. 6. Related provisions: Articles 2, 6(E) and 13.
02
“On and after July 1, 2018, the Board of School Directors may adjust enrollment, boundaries, and school configurations within the Unified District.”
Attendance areas within HUUSD
The authority supports revising the areas used to assign students to district schools, including areas that follow town lines.

A boundary change may assign students to a different HUUSD school.
Scope and related requirements
Article 12 addresses changes within HUUSD.

The first-year parental-consent condition ended June 30, 2018. Choice provisions still apply. Closure requires the Article 6(E) process.
Planning implications: student assignment, transportation, and receiving-school capacity.
Article 12 states: On and after July 1, 2018, the Board of School Directors may adjust enrollment, boundaries, and school configurations within the Unified District. In context, attendance boundaries means the areas used to assign resident students to schools within HUUSD. This is an interpretation of Article 12, which does not define boundaries or specify an assignment formula. The express authority supports revising attendance areas that follow town lines and assigning students to another HUUSD school, subject to other applicable Articles, district policy and law. It does not itself authorize changing HUUSD municipal membership or external district borders. The first-year provision entitled students to attend elementary and middle school according to their town of residence and required parental consent for adjustments between July 1, 2017 and June 30, 2018. That first-year consent condition is not repeated in the ongoing authority beginning July 1, 2018. Choice requirements under Article 13 continue, and a school closure invokes Article 6(E). Article 2 requires the district to provide pre-kindergarten through grade 12 education. Article 3 describes construction needed for the original merger and contains no express permanent prohibition on future construction. Before adopting a specific boundary change, review student assignment policy, transportation, receiving-school capacity, and applicable law.

Required process for K–8 school closure
Article 6(E), pp. 3–4
03
1   Public hearings
At least three hearings about the proposed closure. At least one must occur in the affected community.
2   Notice of intent
After the hearings, if the Board intends to vote, give public notice stating the reason at least ten days before the vote.
3   Board vote
An affirmative vote of two-thirds of the Board of Directors is required.
The initial four-year town-consent provision ended June 30, 2021.
The section heading is Closure of K-8 Schools. Its first sentence prohibited closing schools during the first four years of full operations without consent of the electorate of the town in which the school was located. Operations began July 1, 2017 under Article 10, so that initial period ended June 30, 2021. Thereafter the text requires an affirmative vote of two-thirds of the Board. Do not translate this to a simple number of members or vote-weight points without confirming the interaction with Article 7 and current voting rules. The Articles do not define school closure. Counsel should evaluate grade moves, conversion to another use, or partial program relocation.

Under the supplied Articles, Article 6(E) specifies a Board closure vote after the initial four-year period and does not require a separate closure referendum. Article 11 specifies how budget or public-question votes are conducted, not that every Board action is a public question. 16 V.S.A. § 743(c) provides that bond issues require Australian ballot voting. Under § 722, amendment authority depends on the original and subsequent warnings and any express delegation. Do not assume the Board can change the closure conditions itself. Student placement after a closure
Article 6(E), p. 4
05
Closest district school
Students in the affected town must have the option to attend the district school closest to their residence.
Parental preference
The Articles recognize an alternate preference through parental choice.
Receiving-school capacity
Relocation must also account for available space, classroom needs, and student-to-teacher ratios.
Each option needs a student assignment plan and receiving-school capacity review.
Article 6(E) says students living in a town where a school has closed shall be afforded the option to attend the Unified District school closest to their residence unless parental choice indicates an alternate preference. It also requires consideration of receiving-school space, classroom needs, and student-to-teacher ratios. Present this as an option with operational factors, not an unconditional guarantee of any requested placement. Transportation route impacts are a planning implication, not a separate closure condition expressly stated in Article 6(E).

Intradistrict choice and community input
Articles 13 and 14, p. 6
06
Intradistrict choice
Applies where the district operates a grade in multiple buildings.

Limits must reflect legitimate operational needs or legal requirements.
Choice policy must consider transportation, equity, proximity, siblings, and school capacity.
Article 13 requires policy and programs for intradistrict choice in grades operated in multiple district buildings. Choice may be limited only where necessary to legitimate operational needs and applicable legal requirements. The listed considerations are transportation, socioeconomic equity, proximity, unity of siblings, and capacities of receiving and sending schools. Article 14 requires local input for each operating school building and makes that input advisory. These opportunities complement the specific hearings required by Article 6(E). Do not describe all community meetings as satisfying the formal closure hearing requirement.

Valley elementary properties: offer to the town
Article 6(B), pp. 2–3
07
Trigger
The Board determines the property will no longer serve the direct delivery of student educational programs.
Required offer
Offer the property to the town where it is located for $1, subject to the financial conditions in Article 6(B).
Community use
The town must own and use the property for community and public purposes for at least five years.
A closure decision and the later disposition of property are separate actions.
This provision covers property conveyed by the town elementary forming districts. Applied to the Valley elementary properties, identify each parcel and confirm its chain of ownership. The trigger is a Board determination that the property will not be used in direct delivery of student educational programs. It is not simply a building vacancy or a closure vote. The district shall offer the property to the town where it is located for one dollar, subject to encumbrances, assumption or payment of outstanding bonds and notes, repayment of construction aid or grants required by Vermont law, and costs of capital improvements subsequent to July 1, 2017. A town must own and use the property for community and public purposes for at least five years. If it sells earlier, it must compensate the district for all district-financed capital improvements and renovations prior to the conveyance. If the town declines, sale proceeds pursuant to Vermont statutes on Board-established terms. Adaptive reuse needs review against the direct-delivery trigger.

Property rules differ by former district
Article 6(B), (C) and (D), pp. 2–3
08
Valley elementary
Article 6(B): offer to the town in which the property is located, with specified conditions.
Waterbury–Duxbury
Article 6(D): $1 offer to Waterbury and Duxbury, following the earlier Waterbury–Duxbury agreement.
Former Harwood Union
Article 6(C): statutory sale on Board-established terms when property is incompatible with district operations.
Waterbury–Duxbury analysis requires the incorporated 1994 agreement.
Article 6(C) covers real property conveyed by the Harwood Union High School District. When the district determines the property is incompatible with responsible district operations and educational programs, it shall sell pursuant to Vermont statutes and Board-established terms. Article 6(D) covers property conveyed by the Waterbury/Duxbury Union School District. It requires an offer to Waterbury and Duxbury for one dollar in conformity with the earlier Waterbury-Duxbury Articles, incorporated by reference. Article 6(D) includes financial conditions, five years of community/public use, early-sale compensation, and a sale route if the towns decline. The supplied eight-page PDF references Appendix F, the May 17, 1994 planning report, but does not include it. Obtain that document before evaluating the transfer terms for Brookside or Crossett Brook. Do not apply Valley rules uniformly to these properties.

Original transition provisions have dates
Articles 3–5, 7, 10 and 12, pp. 1–2 and 4–6
10
2017 transition
First-year arrangements and the original debt and asset transfers describe the merger period.
Enrollment protections
The first-year town-based enrollment provision preceded Board configuration authority beginning July 1, 2018.
Board vote weights
Article 7 lists initial 2010 census weights and requires recalculation after each decennial census.
The current voting rules must guide the calculation of a two-thirds closure vote.
Distinguish time-limited founding provisions from ongoing rules. Article 4 describes the first-year transition and existing contracts. Article 5 describes the original transfer of debt, balances and restricted accounts. Some restricted fund conditions may continue, even though the transfer occurred in 2017. Article 6(E) first-four-year protection ended after June 30, 2021. Article 12 first-year town-based enrollment protection preceded Board configuration authority from July 1, 2018. Article 7 shows initial vote weights based on the 2010 census and explicitly requires recalculation after each decennial census. This deck intentionally does not show the initial weights as current weights or convert the two-thirds closure requirement into ten members. Verify current apportionment, voting rules, quorum, vacancies and abstentions with counsel and the clerk.

Questions for each consolidation option
Planning questions derived from Articles 6, 12, 13 and 14
11
Does this option close a school, change its grades, or change its use?
Which students move, and can receiving schools meet their needs?

How will choice, transportation, and local input work?

What property-transfer and financing actions would follow?

What hearings, notices, and votes does the option require?
The Board needs a clear approval sequence for the specific option it selects.
These are suggested Board planning questions, not additional requirements imposed by the Articles. For a specific option, counsel should assess whether it constitutes school closure and which requirements apply. Identify student placement implications, transportation, educational programming, capacity, choice, local input, financing approval and property disposition. Record dates for formal hearings and notice. For multiple closures, identify each school in the proposal, hearing notices, reasons and proposed action. Do not assume general feasibility forums satisfy Article 6(E).
