What is the Yield?
DEFINITION
"The yield is the statewide benchmark used to translate a district's education spending per weighted pupil into a homestead education tax rate."
FY2027 BENCHMARK
$9,401
For FY2027, the property yield is $9,401.
VERMONT LAW DEFINITION
Vermont law defines the property yield as the amount of per-pupil education spending that would produce a $1.00 homestead tax rate per $100 of equalized education property value.
KEY EQUIVALENCE
$9,401 of education spending per weighted pupil  =  $1.00 tax rate
Its basically a statewide checkbook so if Ed Spending statewide it growing more money is needed statewide and the yield is set accordingly. The yield is difficult to estimate as it is driven by the financial status of the education fund and other political factors but also by the Legislature’s management of the fund— including decisions to shift costs into the fund and whether to use reserves. The yield is the lever the legislature has to determine how much money comes into the education fund. When the Legislature lowers the yield, tax rates go up, and if it raises the yield, tax rates go down.

Long Term Weighted Average Daily Membership (LTWADM)
What is LTWADM?
Student Count Adjusted for Need & Time
Core Definition: LTWADM is essentially the district’s student count, averaged over a two-year period.
Weighting Factors: It is adjusted to account for the additional costs associated with serving specific student populations and demographics.
Why LTWADM Matters?
Tax-Capacity & Funding Formula Metric
Beyond Headcount: LTWADM is not simply a raw enrollment number.
Financial Impact: It serves as a key tax-capacity and funding-formula metric that directly determines district spending capacity and property tax calculations.

Key historical and structural factors limiting the District's financial flexibility
Spending Threshold Limits
The last ten years of operating near the excess spending threshold have limited the District's financial flexibility.
Rising Cost of Education
The cost of education has continued to increase, particularly salaries, health insurance, transportation, facilities, and other operating costs.
External Cost Pressures
Many cost increases are outside the District's direct control, while opportunities to reduce costs without affecting students have become increasingly limited.
Significant Prior Reductions
HUUSD has already made significant reductions—approximately $7.2 million over three years and more than 53 positions.
Enrollment & Campus Infrastructure
Enrollment has declined and shifted among our communities, but the District continues to operate seven campus’ with substantial fixed costs.
Incremental reductions alone are no longer sufficient to address the underlying structural pressures.
